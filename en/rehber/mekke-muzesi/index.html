Two Holy Mosques Architecture Exhibition

معرض عمارة الحرمين الشريفين

Museum displaying historic artefacts of the Kaaba and the Prophet's Mosque

Overview

The Two Holy Mosques Architecture Exhibition is in the Umm al-Joud district in western Makkah, near the King Abdulaziz Complex where the covering of the Kaaba (kiswah) is woven. It brings together items that tell the story of about fourteen centuries of architectural development of al-Masjid al-Haram and the Prophet's Mosque. It is run by the General Presidency for the Affairs of the Grand Mosque and the Prophet's Mosque.

The building of about 1,200 square metres has halls for reception, al-Masjid al-Haram, the Kaaba, photography, manuscripts, the Prophet's Mosque and Zamzam. On display are an old wooden pillar of the Kaaba, an old rain spout, a wooden staircase and a door of the Kaaba, models of the two mosques, manuscripts and rare photographs.

History

The exhibition was opened on 1 February 2000 by Prince Abdul Majeed ibn Abdulaziz on behalf of King Fahd. Its purpose was to show visitors the architectural history of the two holy mosques in one place. The building was designed to reflect the architectural style of al-Masjid al-Haram.

Among the Kaaba items are a wooden pillar dated to 65 AH, the time when Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr rebuilt the Kaaba; a rain spout (Mizab al-Rahmah) dated 1021 AH bearing the name of the Ottoman sultan; a wooden staircase dated 1240 AH; and a Kaaba door dated 1363 AH. The Zamzam hall holds pieces of the old equipment of the well.

After a comprehensive renovation, the exhibition entered a trial operation phase in June 2025. The renovation overhauled its technical infrastructure, the way the artefacts are presented and its lighting.

Key Events

  1. 65 AH (684-685)

    The period of the displayed wooden Kaaba pillar: Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr rebuilding the Kaaba.

  2. 1021 AH (1612)

    The displayed Mizab al-Rahmah (Kaaba spout) was made.

  3. 1 February 2000

    The exhibition was opened on behalf of King Fahd.

  4. June 2025

    The renovated exhibition began trial operation; visits are by appointment.

Religious Significance

This is a museum, not a place of worship, and no special virtue is reported for visiting it. Yet seeing how the Kaaba and the Prophet's Mosque were preserved and enlarged over the centuries helps one understand the reverence and effort shown to these places.

Seeing an old door, spout and pillar of the Kaaba up close deepens our knowledge of the history of the House of God that we look at from a distance during tawaf.

Etiquette and Tips

  • Book an appointment in advance at visits.alharamain.gov.sa.
  • The exhibition is about 8-9 km from al-Masjid al-Haram; go by taxi or group vehicle.
  • Ask the staff before taking photographs; do not touch the exhibits.
  • The kiswah factory is nearby; ask your group guide about combining the visits.

Visiting Note

Saudi regulations

The renovated exhibition has been in trial operation since June 2025, and visits are booked through the appointment portal of the Two Holy Mosques authority (visits.alharamain.gov.sa). Opening hours vary by season.

Regulations may change; check the latest rules before your visit.
