Miqat of al-Juhfah

الجحفة

The miqat for those coming from Syria, Egypt and Turkey; the miqat mosque near Rabigh

Overview

Al-Juhfah is an old staging post about 9 km from the Red Sea coast, southeast of the present-day town of Rabigh. It is one of the five miqat points designated by the Prophet: the boundary for entering ihram for those coming from the direction of Syria (al-Sham) and Egypt, and therefore also for pilgrims from Turkey and North Africa who come that way. The hadith states that these miqats apply to their own people and to those of other lands who pass by them.

Today there is a large miqat mosque at al-Juhfah. Pilgrims travelling by road along this route to Makkah perform ghusl here, put on the ihram garments, pray two rak'ahs, make their intention and begin the talbiyah. Most Umrah pilgrims from Turkey, however, fly directly to Jeddah and do not stop at this miqat; their plane crosses the line of the miqat before landing in Jeddah. They therefore enter ihram before the plane reaches that line.

History

According to the sources, al-Juhfah was formerly called Mahya'ah. The tribe of Banu Aqil, driven from their lands by the Amalekites, settled here; when a flood one night swept away their tents and animals, the place came to be called “al-Juhfah”. It was an important stop on the Makkah–Madinah road where pilgrims from Egypt and Syria halted.

According to the narration of Abdullah ibn Abbas, the Prophet designated Dhul-Hulayfah as the miqat for the people of Madinah, al-Juhfah for the people of al-Sham, Qarn al-Manazil for the people of Najd and Yalamlam for the people of Yemen (al-Bukhari, al-Hajj, 7, no. 1524). The authentic sources also record that after the Hijrah, because of Madinah's harsh fever climate, the Prophet prayed to Allah to make Madinah beloved to them and to transfer its fever to al-Juhfah (al-Bukhari, Fada'il al-Madinah, no. 1889).

Over time al-Juhfah became deserted. In the 13th century Yaqut al-Hamawi described it as a ruined place that had once had a Friday mosque. After this period pilgrims began to enter ihram at Rabigh on the coast, which those coming from the north reach before al-Juhfah. Since entering ihram before the miqat is permissible, Rabigh served for a long time as the practical place of ihram for those coming from this direction.

In modern times a miqat mosque has been built at the historic site of al-Juhfah, and it is once again used by road travellers. The TDV Encyclopaedia of Islam notes that the Madinah–Makkah and Jeddah–Makkah highways have reduced the former importance of Rabigh and al-Juhfah.

Key Events

  1. Before Islam

    The place known as Mahya'ah came to be called al-Juhfah after a devastating flood.

  2. After the Hijrah

    The Prophet prayed for the fever of Madinah to be transferred to al-Juhfah.

  3. The Prophet's lifetime

    The Prophet designated al-Juhfah as the miqat for those coming from al-Sham.

  4. 13th century

    Yaqut al-Hamawi described al-Juhfah as a ruin; pilgrims began entering ihram at Rabigh.

  5. Today

    The miqat mosque built at al-Juhfah serves road travellers.

Religious Significance

The miqats are the boundaries at which the servant heading for Allah's House leaves worldly states behind and enters a new state through ihram. These boundaries, set by the Prophet himself, ensure that everyone intending Hajj or Umrah, from whatever direction, approaches the Haram with a certain dignity and preparation.

No special virtue is narrated for al-Juhfah itself; its importance comes from being one of the five miqats designated by the Prophet. Over the centuries countless pilgrims from Egypt, Syria, Anatolia and North Africa began their talbiyah here or in line with it. The verse commanding the completion of Hajj and Umrah for Allah (al-Baqarah 2:196) is a reminder of the sincerity this journey requires.

Etiquette and Tips

  • If you are travelling by road, trim your nails and moustache and attend to personal hygiene before reaching the miqat; perform ghusl at the mosque and put on the ihram garments.
  • If it is not a disliked time for prayer, pray two rak'ahs, then make the intention for Umrah and begin the talbiyah.
  • If you are flying directly to Jeddah, put on the ihram garments at home or at the airport, and make the intention and talbiyah before the plane reaches the line of the miqat. It is more cautious to make the intention a few minutes early rather than risk missing the cabin announcement.
  • Jeddah is not a miqat for those coming from outside; do not postpone ihram until you land in Jeddah. If you have passed the miqat without ihram, consult your group's religious guide.
  • If you go to Madinah first and travel on to Makkah from there, your miqat is not al-Juhfah but Dhul-Hulayfah (Abyar Ali) in Madinah.
  • After entering ihram, observe its prohibitions and repeat the talbiyah often throughout the journey.

Visiting Note

Saudi regulations

The al-Juhfah miqat mosque usually has facilities for wudu and ghusl and areas for changing into ihram; opening hours and facilities may change. The mosque lies southeast of Rabigh and may require leaving the main highway; discuss this in advance with your bus driver and guide. For detailed rulings on ihram, consult a reliable Hajj and Umrah guide and your group's religious guide.

Regulations may change; check the latest rules before your visit.
