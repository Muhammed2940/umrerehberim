Birthplace of the Prophet (Makkah Library)

مكتبة مكة المكرمة

A library now stands where the Prophet is traditionally held to have been born

Overview

On the eastern square of al-Masjid al-Haram, right across from the Mas'a building between Safa and Marwah, you will see a plain two-storey building. The sign above its door reads “Makkah al-Mukarramah Library”. This is the place reported in the sources as the house where the Prophet was born. Most historians place it in Shi'b Abi Talib (Shi'b Bani Hashim), the quarter where the Hashim clan once lived.

The houses and markets around it were removed during the expansions of the Haram, so the building now stands alone on the square. It measures about 24 by 13 metres and is about 10 metres high. The library belongs to the Ministry of Islamic Affairs, Dawah and Guidance. The building is usually closed to visitors; pilgrims see it from outside.

Scholars differ on whether this is exactly the spot where the Prophet was born. It should therefore be seen as a place of remembrance that has been known as such for centuries, though its precise identification is debated.

History

According to the commonly accepted view, the Prophet was born in Makkah in the month of Rabi al-Awwal in 571, the Year of the Elephant. The sources report that the house belonged to his grandfather Abd al-Muttalib, who divided it among his sons.

When the Prophet emigrated to Madinah, the house passed into the hands of his cousin Aqil ibn Abi Talib. It is narrated that at the conquest of Makkah, when he was asked whether he would stay in his house, he said, “Has Aqil left us any house?” and did not reclaim it. According to the sources, the house was later sold to Muhammad ibn Yusuf, the brother of al-Hajjaj. Al-Khayzuran, the mother of the Abbasid caliph Harun al-Rashid, separated the part where the birth took place and turned it into a mosque.

The mosque was renewed many times in the Abbasid, Mamluk and Ottoman periods. The present building is thought to rest on an older plan from the 10th century AH. In the 20th century Sheikh Abbas Qattan, the mayor of Makkah, built a new building under the patronage of King Abdulaziz, and the library officially opened in 1370 AH (1951). Its core was the book collection of Sheikh Muhammad Tahir al-Kurdi. Today it holds private collections of Makkah scholars, manuscripts and early printed books.

Key Events

  1. 571 (Year of the Elephant)

    According to the common view, the Prophet was born in this quarter in Rabi al-Awwal.

  2. After 622

    After the Hijrah the house passed to Aqil ibn Abi Talib.

  3. 2nd century AH (8th century)

    Al-Khayzuran turned the part known as the birthplace into a mosque.

  4. 1370 AH (1951)

    The Makkah Library officially opened in the building built by Sheikh Abbas Qattan.

Religious Significance

No act of worship or special virtue is reported for this place. There is no particular prayer, supplication or custom to perform here. Its significance lies in reminding us of the quarter where the Prophet, sent as a mercy to the worlds, was born.

Standing here is a good opportunity to picture Makkah as it was then, to remember how a child born an orphan became a prophet who guided all humanity, to send blessings upon him and to renew one's commitment to his Sunnah.

Etiquette and Tips

  • The building is usually closed; it is visited by looking at it from the square.
  • Do not touch the walls, door or windows seeking blessing; follow the instructions of the staff.
  • There is no special prayer here; sending blessings upon the Prophet is good anywhere.
  • The square is very crowded at prayer times; keep your stop short and do not block walkways.

Visiting Note

Saudi regulations

The building is used as a library and is generally not open to visitors. Information boards and staff are sometimes present in front of it. The square may change because of development works around the Haram.

Regulations may change; check the latest rules before your visit.
