Khaybar

خيبر

The oasis and fortresses where the Expedition of Khaybar took place

Overview

Khaybar is the name of a broad valley, surrounded by volcanic lava fields (harrah), about 180 km north of Madinah at an altitude of 850–1,000 m, and of the oasis within it. It was known for its abundant springs, its palm groves and its strong fortresses. It was an important trading centre on the main north–south route of the Arabian Peninsula, and a large fair was held here every year after the Hajj season.

Today the remains of the old fortresses in the oasis are protected as archaeological sites. The best known is the Qamus Fort, presented as the fortress conquered by Ali during the Expedition of Khaybar. Because the old names of the fortresses were forgotten over time, the locations of some of them are not known with certainty. The oasis also has ancient dams and traces of settlement.

History

The earliest information about Khaybar is found in an inscription of Nabonidus, the last king of Babylon (556–539 BCE). At the time of the rise of Islam, Khaybar was home to prosperous Jewish communities living in fortified settlements made up of separate fortresses; the Banu Nadir, who had left Madinah, had also settled here.

The leaders of Khaybar had formed an alliance against Madinah with the Makkan polytheists and some of the surrounding tribes; the Battle of the Trench (5 AH / 627) was the result of this alliance. After peace with the Quraysh was secured by the Treaty of Hudaybiyah, the Prophet marched on Khaybar in less than a month with a force of about 1,500 men (7 AH / 628).

The fortresses were taken one by one. The Qamus fortress in the town was conquered through the outstanding effort of Ali, while some fortresses surrendered peacefully. The Prophet spared the lives of the inhabitants and allowed them to remain on their land as sharecroppers in return for half of the harvest. The sources also report that copies of the Torah found among the spoils were returned to their owners. The Jewish community of Khaybar lived there until it was sent to the regions of Syria during the caliphate of Umar (20 AH / 641).

After the conquest of Khaybar, the Prophet freed Safiyyah, the daughter of Huyayy ibn Akhtab, who was among the captives, and married her; Safiyyah became one of the Mothers of the Believers. It was also at Khaybar that poisoned meat was offered to the Prophet; he spat out the first mouthful at once, but Bishr ibn al-Bara, who ate with him, died.

Religious Significance

The conquest of Khaybar has been associated with the glad tidings of “an imminent victory” and “many spoils that you will take” in Surah al-Fath, revealed on the way back from al-Hudaybiyah; some exegetes say that the promise in verse 20 of Surah al-Fath includes Khaybar.

According to a narration in al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet said at Khaybar that he would give the banner to a man who loves Allah and His Messenger and whom Allah and His Messenger love, and the next day he gave it to Ali. Khaybar is also an example of the justice shown to a conquered people: their lives were spared, they were allowed to remain on their land, and their holy scriptures were returned to them.

Etiquette and Tips

  • The fortress remains are historical and archaeological sites; do not climb on the ruins or take stones or objects.
  • It is a long journey; start a day trip from Madinah early in the morning.
  • Wear sturdy, closed shoes on the volcanic, rocky ground.
  • Speak about these historical events in a respectful and measured way; Khaybar is also a place where the justice shown to a conquered people is remembered.
  • Carry water, a hat and sunscreen; temperatures are very high in summer.

Visiting Note

Saudi regulations

The fortress remains at Khaybar are protected archaeological sites; some parts may be closed to visitors because of restoration or excavation work. As Khaybar is far from Madinah, people usually go by private tour or car; it is not always included in group programmes.

Regulations may change; check the latest rules before your visit.
