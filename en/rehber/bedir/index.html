Badr

بدر

Site of the Battle of Badr, the Arish Mosque and the Martyrs' Cemetery

Overview

Badr is a small town about 160 km southwest of Madinah and some 30 km from the Red Sea coast. In the past it was a staging post known for its wells, where the Makkah–Madinah road met the caravan route to Syria; every year a fair lasting eight days was held here at the beginning of the month of Dhul-Qa'dah. Today it is the centre of Badr Governorate in the Madinah Province.

The main places visitors stop at are the Arish Mosque, believed to stand where the shelter (arish) that served as the Prophet's command post during the battle was set up; the Badr Martyrs' Cemetery, where the fourteen Companions martyred in the battle rest; and the plain where the battle took place. A plaque in front of the cemetery lists the names of the martyrs.

History

In the second year of the Hijrah a large Quraysh trade caravan led by Abu Sufyan was returning from Syria. On 12 Ramadan (8 March 624) the Prophet set out from Madinah with about 305 men (313 in some sources); the force had seventy camels and two horses. Learning of this, Abu Sufyan took the coastal road, saved the caravan and called on Makkah for help. An army of about 1,000 men under Abu Jahl set out and, even after hearing that the caravan was safe, kept marching towards Badr to show its strength.

The Qur'an states that the two armies arrived at Badr unaware of each other (al-Anfal 8:42). Heavy rain on the night before the battle halted both sides. The Prophet reached the wells before the Quraysh and took up position on the advice of Hubab ibn al-Mundhir. Before the battle he sent Umar as an envoy, offering the Quraysh to return to Makkah without fighting, but they insisted on battle.

On 17 Ramadan 2 AH (13 March 624) the battle opened with single combats in which Hamzah, Ali and Ubaydah ibn al-Harith took part, and it ended towards the afternoon with a decisive victory for the Muslims. Seventy of the polytheists, including Abu Jahl, were killed and seventy were taken captive; the Muslims lost fourteen martyrs. The Prophet led the funeral prayer for the martyrs and had them buried; he also had the dead of the Quraysh buried.

The Prophet ordered that the captives be treated well. Most were released for a ransom and some without any payment, while those who could read and write were freed on condition that each taught ten Muslims to read and write. The victory at Badr earned the Muslim community of Madinah great standing throughout the Arabian Peninsula.

Key Events

  1. 12 Ramadan 2 AH / 8 March 624

    The Prophet set out from Madinah with about 305 men.

  2. The night before the battle

    Heavy rain fell and both armies stayed where they were.

  3. 17 Ramadan 2 AH / 13 March 624, morning

    The Muslims reached the wells first; Umar was sent to the Quraysh with an offer of peace, which was refused.

  4. 17 Ramadan 2 AH, during the day

    The battle, which began with single combats, ended towards the afternoon in victory for the Muslims; fourteen Companions were martyred.

  5. After the battle

    Some captives were freed for ransom; those who could read and write were freed in return for teaching ten Muslims each.

  6. End of Ramadan – early Shawwal 2 AH

    The Prophet returned to Madinah with his army.

Religious Significance

Badr is a battle mentioned by name in the Qur'an. Surah Al Imran reminds the believers that Allah helped them at Badr when they were few and weak, and Surah al-Anfal states that they were reinforced with angels. The Qur'an calls that day “the Day of the Criterion” (Yawm al-Furqan), the day on which truth was distinguished from falsehood (al-Anfal 8:41).

The Companions who took part in Badr are called “the people of Badr” (Ahl Badr) and hold a special rank among the Companions. The hadith collections contain narrations giving the glad tidings that the people of Badr were forgiven (al-Bukhari, al-Maghazi, 9; Muslim, Fada'il al-Sahabah, 161). A visit to Badr is an occasion to reflect on how a small group of believers who acted with patience and trust in Allah attained His help.

Etiquette and Tips

  • When you reach the cemetery, greet the martyrs with salam; recite al-Fatihah and al-Ikhlas and pray for the martyrs and all believers.
  • The visit is for reflection and prayer; avoid tying cloth to the railings or rubbing your face on the graves.
  • If it is prayer time, pray at the Arish Mosque, and keep your voice low.
  • Badr is far from Madinah; plan the visit as a day trip, setting off early in the morning.
  • Temperatures are very high in summer; carry water, a hat and sunscreen.
  • While touring the battlefield, listen to the related verses and the Sirah from your guide and reflect on the events.

Visiting Note

Saudi regulations

The Badr Martyrs' Cemetery is enclosed by a wall and railings; visitors usually stand in front of the railings. Under Saudi practice there may be restrictions on women visiting cemeteries. The Arish Mosque is reported to be closed at times outside prayer times.

Regulations may change; check the latest rules before your visit.
