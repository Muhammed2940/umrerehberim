The Trench and the Seven Mosques (Masjid al-Fath)

المساجد السبعة

The foot of Mount Sal', scene of the Battle of the Trench

Overview

Al-Masajid as-Sab'ah (the Seven Mosques) is the common name for the small mosques at the north-western foot of Mount Sal' in Madinah, in the area where the Battle of the Trench took place. Of these, the mosques of al-Fath, Salman al-Farisi, Ali and Abu Bakr are mentioned in classical sources, while those of Umar and Sa'd ibn Mu'adh (also called Fatimah's mosque) are thought to have been built later. The name “Seven Mosques” itself became common in recent times.

The most important building in the area is Masjid al-Fath, at the place where the Prophet's tent was pitched and where he prayed during the siege of the Trench. Because it stands on higher ground it is also called al-Masjid al-A'la, and after the confederate groups (al-Ahzab) it is called Masjid al-Ahzab. Some of the small mosques have been demolished in recent years, and the large al-Khandaq Mosque has been built beside them.

History

In the 5th year of the Hijrah (627), Quraysh, Ghatafan and their allied tribes (al-Ahzab) marched on Madinah with an army of about 10,000-12,000. The Prophet consulted his Companions and, according to most sources at the suggestion of Salman al-Farisi, it was decided to dig a trench on the open northern side of the city. The Prophet and his Companions completed the trench, about 5.5 km long, in a few weeks, with the Prophet himself digging.

The Muslim army of about 3,000 camped at the foot of Mount Sal'; the Prophet moved his tent to the place where Masjid al-Fath now stands. The siege lasted about a month. The enemy could not cross the trench; when Amr ibn Abd Wudd once crossed it, Ali defeated him in single combat. Through the stratagem of Nu'aym ibn Mas'ud, discord arose among the enemy ranks. Finally a bitter cold and wind scattered the confederates; Abu Sufyan lifted the siege and returned to Makkah.

A mosque with a single portico was first built at the place where the Prophet prayed. Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz rebuilt it, and it was renewed in 1179 under the Ayyubids. Today's Masjid al-Fath is the small mosque rebuilt in basalt by Sultan Abdulmejid in 1853-54.

Key Events

  1. Shawwal 5 AH (March 627)

    At Salman al-Farisi's suggestion the trench was dug; the Prophet took part in the digging himself.

  2. The siege

    The confederate army besieged Madinah for about a month; the Prophet pitched his tent where Masjid al-Fath now stands.

  3. During the siege

    Ali defeated Amr ibn Abd Wudd, who had crossed the trench, in single combat.

  4. Dhu al-Qa'dah 5 AH (April 627)

    Cold and a violent wind scattered the confederates; the siege was lifted.

  5. 1853-1854

    Sultan Abdulmejid rebuilt Masjid al-Fath in basalt.

Religious Significance

This area holds the memory of the Battle of the Trench, when Allah showed His help to the believers at a difficult moment. Surat al-Ahzab describes those days when eyes shifted in fear and hearts reached the throats, and tells that Allah sent against the enemy a wind and armies that could not be seen. When the believers saw the confederates they said, “This is what Allah and His Messenger promised us,” and it only increased them in faith and submission.

No authentic hadith is known about a special reward specific to Masjid al-Fath; the visit is to remember the patience, consultation and prayer the Prophet showed in those hard days.

Etiquette and Tips

  • On arrival you can pray two rak'ahs in the large al-Khandaq Mosque or in Masjid al-Fath, if open.
  • Some of the small mosques may be closed; avoid gathering in front of closed buildings.
  • Masjid al-Fath is reached by stairs; the elderly and those with walking difficulties should take care.
  • Remember here that the Prophet worked alongside his Companions digging the trench and endured hunger and cold with patience.
