Masjid al-Jinn

مسجد الجن

Near Jannat al-Mu'alla; remembered as the place where the jinn listened to the Qur'an

Overview

Masjid al-Jinn is in the al-Hajun district, north of al-Masjid al-Haram, close to Jannat al-Mu'alla, the historic cemetery of Makkah. According to the sources, one night the Prophet came to this area with Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, drew a line on the ground, asked him not to cross it, and recited the Qur'an to the jinn beyond the line. The place was therefore first called “the place of the line”, and the mosque later built here was named Masjid al-Jinn.

It is also known as the Mosque of the Guards, because night watchmen used to gather here to change shifts. Since it is also related that the jinn pledged allegiance to the Prophet here, it has been called the Mosque of the Pledge as well; however, a separate mosque at the site of the 'Aqabah pledges bears the same name. The present building is a modern mosque with a minaret, entirely rebuilt in 2000.

History

The Prophet recited the Qur'an to the jinn at different times and places in order to convey the revelation to them. The Qur'an tells, in verse 29 of Surat al-Ahqaf and at the beginning of Surat al-Jinn, that a group of jinn listened to the Qur'an and believed. The Makkan historian al-Azraqi relates that the Prophet came near al-Hajun with Abdullah ibn Mas'ud and recited the Qur'an to the jinn beyond the line he had drawn. The mosque was built where this memory lives on.

Al-Azraqi and al-Fakihi, writing in the ninth century, mention a place where the Prophet met the jinn, but say nothing of a building there. In the sixteenth century the place was identified with a spot called al-Farhadiyyah near Jannat al-Mu'alla. Every year, three days before the end of Dhu al-Hijjah, the people of Makkah gathered here and spent the night. The Moroccan traveller al-'Ayyashi, who saw it in 1662, writes that the hill was wooded and that food was distributed to the poor.

The first mosque building was erected in 1700 by an architect named Ibrahim Agha, between two wide streets, below ground level, without a dome or minaret. In 2000 the mosque was entirely rebuilt; today it stands above ground as a modern mosque with a minaret and, together with Jannat al-Mu'alla, is one of the most visited places in Makkah.

Key Events

  1. Makkan period

    The Prophet recited the Qur'an to the jinn near al-Hajun, accompanied by Abdullah ibn Mas'ud (as related by al-Azraqi).

  2. 9th century

    Al-Azraqi and al-Fakihi mention the place; no building is yet recorded.

  3. 1662

    The traveller al-'Ayyashi writes that food was given to the poor on the wooded hill called al-Farhadiyyah.

  4. 1700

    The architect Ibrahim Agha built the first mosque below ground, without dome or minaret.

  5. 2000

    The mosque was rebuilt above ground as a modern mosque with a minaret.

Religious Significance

Masjid al-Jinn is a reminder that the Prophet's mission was not only to humankind but also to the jinn. The Qur'an relates that a group of jinn listened to the Qur'an, said “We have heard a wondrous Qur'an”, and believed.

There is no authentic hadith attributing a special virtue to this mosque. It is visited to recall an episode from the Prophet's life, and to pray and supplicate there.

Etiquette and Tips

  • The mosque is on the same road as Jannat al-Mu'alla; both can be visited on one trip.
  • It is about a 15-20 minute walk from al-Masjid al-Haram; in the heat a taxi may be better.
  • On entering you may pray two rak‘ahs to greet the mosque, and recite Surat al-Jinn and reflect on its meaning.
  • Pay no attention to baseless popular tales about the jinn; the theme of the visit is the conveying of the Qur'an.
  • The mosque may be closed outside prayer times; it is better to go close to a prayer time.
