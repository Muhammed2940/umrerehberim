Dhul-Hulayfah (Abyar Ali) and Masjid ash-Shajarah

ميقات ذي الحليفة

The miqat of Madinah: where pilgrims enter ihram on the way to Makkah

Overview

Dhul-Hulayfah is the miqat where pilgrims travelling to Makkah from Madinah, or from the direction of Madinah, enter ihram. It lies south-west of Madinah in Wadi al-Aqiq, about 11 km from the Prophet's Mosque. At about 420 km by road from Makkah, it is the farthest of the miqats. Because of the wells attributed to Ali here, it is popularly known as “Abyar Ali” (the wells of Ali).

The large mosque at the miqat is called Masjid ash-Shajarah (Mosque of the Tree) because it was built on the site of the samurah tree under which the Prophet prayed; it is also called the Miqat Mosque and the Ihram Mosque. Enlarged under King Fahd, the complex covers some 90,000 m²; its prayer hall of 6,000 m² can hold about 5,000 worshippers at once. The complex has many facilities for ghusl and wudu, separate sections for women and shops selling ihram items.

History

The Prophet designated Dhul-Hulayfah as the miqat for the people of Madinah (al-Bukhari, Hajj, 7). He passed through it on all four of his journeys from Madinah to Makkah after the Hijrah. In the year 6 AH (628), when he set out for Umrah, he marked his sacrificial animals here, entered ihram and prayed under a samurah tree; that journey ended with the Treaty of al-Hudaybiyah. The following year he again entered ihram here for the Umrah of al-Qada. On his way to the conquest of Makkah, however, he did not enter ihram here, so as to keep his objective secret.

In the year 10 AH, setting out from Madinah for the Farewell Pilgrimage on 25 Dhul-Qa'dah, the Prophet prayed the noon prayer of four rak'ahs in his mosque and the afternoon prayer of two rak'ahs at Dhul-Hulayfah, and spent the night here. The next day he performed ghusl, entered ihram and set off reciting the talbiyah. The road from Madinah to this place was called the Road of the Tree, and the return road the Road of al-Mu'arras.

The place of the tree under which the Prophet prayed was marked and protected. While governor of Madinah, Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz turned it into the Mosque of the Tree. In the Abbasid period it was a simple building of stone and mud brick. Evliya Çelebi describes a mosque with twelve domes and no minaret, and a well attributed to Ali. The mosque was often damaged by floods. As the number of pilgrims grew it was enlarged in the mid-twentieth century, and after work under King Faisal, the present large complex was built under King Fahd.

Key Events

  1. 6 AH (628)

    The Prophet entered ihram here for Umrah; the journey ended with the Treaty of al-Hudaybiyah.

  2. 7 AH (629)

    Ihram was again entered here for the Umrah of al-Qada.

  3. 25-26 Dhul-Qa'dah 10 AH (632)

    On the Farewell Pilgrimage the Prophet spent the night here, performed ghusl, entered ihram and set off with the talbiyah.

  4. 706-712

    Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz built the Mosque of the Tree on the site of the tree.

  5. Reign of King Fahd

    The present large miqat complex was built.

Religious Significance

Dhul-Hulayfah is one of the five miqats designated by the Prophet himself, and the place where he entered ihram on the Farewell Pilgrimage. Since it is the farthest miqat from Makkah, it has also been said that entering ihram here carries greater reward. Every pilgrim travelling from Madinah to Makkah enters ihram here, following in the footsteps of the Prophet.

Ihram is the symbol of shedding worldly clothes and adornments and coming before Allah as equal servants. The first talbiyah said here, “Here I am, O Allah, here I am!”, is the answer to the invitation of the Lord.

Etiquette and Tips

  • Before ihram, trim your nails and moustache and attend to personal hygiene; if possible, perform ghusl at the hotel. There are ghusl and wudu facilities at the miqat too, but they can be crowded.
  • Men wear the two unstitched pieces of ihram cloth, leave their heads uncovered and wear sandals that leave the ankles exposed. Women enter ihram in their normal modest clothing, with face and hands uncovered.
  • Perfume may be applied to the body before ihram, but not to the ihram cloth. If it is not a disliked time for prayer, pray two rak'ahs for ihram.
  • Then make the intention for Umrah and recite the talbiyah: “Labbayk Allahumma labbayk, labbayka la sharika laka labbayk, inna al-hamda wa an-ni'mata laka wa al-mulk, la sharika lak.” The prohibitions of ihram begin from this moment.
  • If you travel by bus, groups usually stop at the miqat mosque; note your meeting point and bus number. Keep an eye on your valuables and sandals.

Visiting Note

Saudi regulations

The Haramain high-speed train does not stop at Dhul-Hulayfah. Those travelling by train should put on their ihram clothing in Madinah and make the intention and talbiyah before reaching the line of the miqat; an announcement may be made on the train. Those flying from Madinah to Jeddah must also enter ihram before passing the miqat. Arrangements may change; follow the instructions of your agency and religious guide.

Regulations may change; check the latest rules before your visit.
