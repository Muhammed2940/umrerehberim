MakkahHaram

Masjid al-Haram and the Kaaba

المسجد الحرام والكعبة المشرفة

The Kaaba, the Black Stone, Maqam Ibrahim, the Hijr, Zamzam, Safa and Marwah

Overview

Masjid al-Haram is the place of worship in the heart of Makkah that surrounds the Kaaba, regarded as the holiest mosque on earth. Muslims everywhere face the Kaaba at its centre in their five daily prayers, and the core rites of Umrah and Hajj, such as tawaf and sa'i, are performed here. The Qur'an calls the Kaaba al-Bayt, Baytullah, al-Bayt al-Atiq and al-Bayt al-Haram.

The Kaaba is a cube-shaped building of roughly 10.70 × 12 metres and 15 metres high, built of stone brought from the mountains around Makkah. At its eastern corner is the Black Stone (al-Hajar al-Aswad), where tawaf begins; in front of its north-western wall is the Hijr, enclosed by a low semicircular wall (al-Hatim). The roughly two-metre stretch between the door and the Black Stone is called al-Multazam. Facing the Kaaba stands the Maqam Ibrahim, the stone believed to bear the footprints of Prophet Ibrahim; nearby is the Well of Zamzam, and on the eastern side of the mosque are the hills of Safa and Marwah, where sa'i is performed. Today the sa'i course (al-Mas'a) lies inside the mosque; the distance between Safa and Marwah is about 394.5 metres.

History

According to the Qur'an, the Kaaba is the first house of worship established for mankind; Prophet Ibrahim and his son Ismail raised its foundations. Hajar, the wife of Ibrahim, went back and forth seven times between Safa and Marwah in search of water for her son, after which the spring of Zamzam gushed forth. The rite of sa'i keeps the memory of this event alive. When water appeared, the tribe of Jurhum settled here and the city of Makkah was born. The well was later buried and lost, and centuries afterwards it was re-dug by Abd al-Muttalib, the Prophet's grandfather.

The Kaaba was rebuilt several times in history. When the Quraysh rebuilt it in 605, a dispute arose over who would set the Black Stone back in its place. Muhammad, who had not yet received prophethood, placed the stone on a cloth, had the tribal chiefs lift it together by its edges, and set it in place with his own hands. Because the funds gathered from lawful earnings were not enough, the Quraysh left part of Ibrahim's foundations outside the building; this is the Hijr.

In 684 Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr rebuilt the Kaaba on Ibrahim's foundations, and in 692 al-Hajjaj restored some parts to their former shape. In 930 the Qarmatians carried off the Black Stone; it was returned about twenty years later. When a great flood in 1630 brought down the Kaaba's walls, the building was faithfully rebuilt in the reign of the Ottoman Sultan Murad IV, and the present structure largely dates from that reconstruction.

The mosque itself has been enlarged many times since the caliphate of Umar. The domed Ottoman arcades seen today were built in 1576. From 1955 onwards, major Saudi expansions brought the course between Safa and Marwah inside the mosque, added several storeys and enlarged it to hold millions of worshippers.

Key Events

  1. Time of Prophet Ibrahim

    Hajar's running between Safa and Marwah and the emergence of Zamzam; Ibrahim and Ismail raise the foundations of the Kaaba.

  2. 605

    The Quraysh rebuild the Kaaba; Muhammad settles the dispute over the Black Stone.

  3. AH 8 (630)

    The conquest of Makkah: the Kaaba is cleansed of idols, the Prophet prays inside and hands the key back to Uthman ibn Talha.

  4. 638

    Caliph Umar buys the surrounding houses, enlarges the mosque for the first time and encloses it with a wall.

  5. 684 and 692

    Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr rebuilds the Kaaba on Ibrahim's foundations; al-Hajjaj restores some parts to their former shape.

  6. 930–951

    The Qarmatians carry off the Black Stone; it is returned about twenty years later.

  7. 1576

    Under the Ottomans, the flat wooden roof is replaced by the domed arcades still seen today.

  8. 1630–1631

    A great flood brings down the Kaaba's walls; it is faithfully rebuilt in the reign of Sultan Murad IV.

  9. 1955 onwards

    Saudi expansions: the course between Safa and Marwah is brought inside, and the mosque becomes multi-storey.

Religious Significance

The Kaaba is the qibla of Muslims, and the Qur'an calls it the first house of worship established for mankind. Hajj to this house is made obligatory for those able to undertake it. The Prophet stated that a prayer in Masjid al-Haram is better than a hundred thousand prayers elsewhere.

The Qur'an commands that the Station of Ibrahim be taken as a place of prayer; after tawaf, two rak'ahs are prayed behind it when possible. Safa and Marwah are counted in the Qur'an among the symbols of Allah. Of Zamzam the Prophet said that it serves the purpose for which it is drunk.

Etiquette and Tips

  • Enter with your right foot, with the supplication for entering a mosque and with calm; make whatever supplication you wish on first seeing the Kaaba.
  • Begin tawaf in line with the Black Stone; do not push to touch it in crowds—pointing to it from a distance is enough.
  • After tawaf pray two rak'ahs behind the Station of Ibrahim, or anywhere suitable in the mosque if it is crowded.
  • Drink Zamzam facing the qibla, saying Bismillah and making supplication; take bottles only from the permitted dispensing points.
  • Do not walk in front of people praying, do not sit in the walkways, and follow the directions of the staff.
  • Agree on a gate number as a meeting point in case you get separated; the gates of the mosque are numbered.

Visiting Note

Saudi regulations

At busy times the ground-floor tawaf area around the Kaaba (mataf) may be reserved for those performing Umrah; others are directed to the upper floors. Prams may not be allowed into the mataf. During peak periods such as Ramadan, a Nusuk permit may be required for some activities. There is sometimes a protective barrier around the Black Stone and the cover of the Kaaba.

Regulations may change; check the latest rules before your visit.
