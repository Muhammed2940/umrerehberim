Tabuk: Masjid al-Tawbah and Tabuk Castle

تبوك

The city of the Tabuk expedition, the Prophet's last campaign

Overview

Tabuk is a city in the northwest of Saudi Arabia, near the Jordanian border, on the old Madinah–Syria trade route. It entered Islamic history through the Tabuk expedition of the ninth year of the Hijrah, the last campaign the Prophet led in person, against the Byzantines.

Two buildings standing side by side in the old centre attract visitors. Masjid al-Tawbah is a mosque which local tradition holds was built where the Prophet prayed during his stay at Tabuk; locals also call it “the Prophet's Mosque”. Close by stands Tabuk Castle, one of the stations of the old pilgrim road; it was renovated in the Ottoman period and is now visited as a small museum.

History

In Rajab of the ninth year of the Hijrah (October 630), on hearing that the Byzantines were preparing against the Muslims in northern Arabia, the Prophet announced a campaign. Because it coincided with a time of scarcity and intense heat, it was called “Jaysh al-Usrah”, the army of hardship. Uthman equipped a large part of the army with 300 camels and 1,000 dinars; Abu Bakr brought all his wealth and Umar half of his. An army of about 30,000 set out for Tabuk.

On the way they passed through al-Hijr, the land of Thamud. The army stayed at Tabuk for fifteen to twenty days, but the Byzantine army did not come out to meet them and there was no battle. During this time Yuhanna ibn Ru'bah, the bishop of Aylah (Aqaba), and the people of Jarba, Adhruh and Maqna made treaties agreeing to pay the jizyah, and the Prophet had covenants written guaranteeing the safety of their lives and property. Khalid ibn al-Walid was sent to Dumat al-Jandal with 400 horsemen, and its ruler Ukaydir also made a treaty.

In Sahih Muslim, Mu'adh ibn Jabal relates that when the army reached the spring of Tabuk it was yielding very little water. The Prophet washed his hands and face with some of its water and had it poured back into the spring; the spring began to flow abundantly and everyone drew what they needed. The Prophet then said: “O Mu'adh, if you live long, you will see this place filled with gardens” (Muslim, al-Fada'il, 10, no. 706).

After a campaign of about fifty days the army returned to Madinah in Ramadan (December 630). Three Companions who had stayed behind without excuse, Ka'b ibn Malik, Murarah ibn al-Rabi' and Hilal ibn Umayyah, went through a trial of fifty days during which no one spoke to them; finally the acceptance of their repentance was revealed in verses 117-118 of Surah al-Tawbah (al-Bukhari, al-Maghazi, no. 4418; Muslim, al-Tawbah, no. 2769).

According to the sources, the mosque at Tabuk was first built of stone and mud brick during Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz's governorship of Madinah (706–712), and was renewed in Ottoman times in 1062 AH (1651-52) and in the period of the Hejaz Railway. The present building was rebuilt in the style of the Prophet's Mosque by order of King Faisal in 1393–1395 AH (1973–1975). Some historians and scholars do not regard the direct attribution of the mosque to the Prophet as certain. The castle beside it is recorded to have been renewed in the reign of Suleiman the Magnificent in 967 AH (1559-60).

Key Events

  1. Rajab 9 AH / October 630

    The “army of hardship”, about 30,000 strong, set out from Madinah for Tabuk.

  2. On the way

    The army passed through al-Hijr; the Prophet ordered that its water not be used.

  3. 15–20 days at Tabuk

    There was no battle; treaties were made with Aylah, Jarba, Adhruh and Maqna, and Khalid ibn al-Walid was sent to Dumat al-Jandal.

  4. Ramadan 9 AH / December 630

    The army returned to Madinah after a campaign of about fifty days.

  5. Fifty days after the return

    The acceptance of the repentance of the three Companions who stayed behind was revealed in al-Tawbah 9:117-118.

  6. 706–712

    According to the sources, the Tabuk mosque was first built in the time of Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz.

  7. 967 AH / 1559-60

    Tabuk Castle was renewed in the reign of Suleiman the Magnificent.

  8. 1393–1395 AH / 1973–1975

    Masjid al-Tawbah was rebuilt in its present form by order of King Faisal.

Religious Significance

Tabuk is the name of a campaign to which the Qur'an devotes a long passage. A large part of Surah al-Tawbah was revealed in connection with it: those who spent their means in the way of Allah are praised, the state of the hypocrites who made excuses and stayed behind is exposed, and it is announced that Allah accepted the repentance of the Emigrants and Helpers who followed the Prophet “in the hour of hardship” (al-Tawbah 9:117).

The most moving lesson of Tabuk is the story of Ka'b ibn Malik and his two companions. Instead of inventing a false excuse they told the truth, endured fifty days of painful isolation with patience, and in the end Allah accepted their repentance (al-Tawbah 9:118). This story, told by Ka'b himself, is related at length in the authentic hadith collections. A visit to Tabuk is an occasion to remember the value of truthfulness, sacrifice and sincere repentance. The name “al-Tawbah” (repentance) given to the mosque keeps this memory alive.

Etiquette and Tips

  • If it is prayer time, pray at Masjid al-Tawbah, and reflect on the lessons of patience and sacrifice of the Tabuk campaign.
  • The direct attribution of the mosque to the Prophet is not certain; avoid attaching special rites to it in hope of blessing, or rubbing your face on its walls.
  • On the occasion of your visit, read verses 117-118 of Surah al-Tawbah and renew your own repentance.
  • Do not touch the objects displayed in the castle, and follow the staff's instructions.
  • Tabuk's climate is cold in winter and hot in summer; dress for the season.

Visiting Note

Saudi regulations

Tabuk is very far from Makkah and Madinah and is not usually part of Umrah programmes; a visit requires a separate road trip or flight. Masjid al-Tawbah is reported to be closed at times outside prayer times, and the castle museum to be open only at set hours; check the current hours before visiting.

Regulations may change; check the latest rules before your visit.
