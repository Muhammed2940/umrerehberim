Gates of the Haram

The main gates of Masjid al-Haram and Masjid an-Nabawi, their numbers and locations.

Gate usage (entry/exit, women-only gates, closures) may be changed by the authorities depending on crowds and expansion works. Follow the staff and the signs at the gate.

Masjid al-Haram20 gates

  • Gate 1King Abdulaziz GateSouth side · facing the Clock Tower (Abraj al-Bait)باب الملك عبد العزيز+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after King Abdulaziz Al Saud, founder of Saudi Arabia. It is Gate 1 and one of the five main gates.
    Location and nearby
    On the southern façade, on the Yemeni Corner side, directly opposite Ajyad Street and the Clock Tower (Abraj al-Bait). It is the first big gate you see when leaving the Clock Tower hotels.
    Practical tip
    One of the gates giving wheelchair users direct access to the mataf. Main gates are marked by two minarets above them.
  • Gate 3Ajyad GateSouth side · Ajyad district and hotelsباب أجياد+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after the Ajyad district and valley that it faces.
    Location and nearby
    On the southern façade just east of King Abdulaziz Gate; close to the Ajyad and Clock Tower hotels.
    Practical tip
    In 2025 it was among the gates assigned for Umrah pilgrims to enter the mataf (with King Fahd 79 and Umrah 62).
  • Gate 11Al-Safa GateEast-southeast · Safa hill, start of Sa‘iباب الصفا+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after Mount Safa, which it faces. "Safa" refers to smooth, clear stone.
    Location and nearby
    On the Safa hill side, at the southern end of the Mas‘a (between Safa and Marwah). It leads to Safa, where Sa‘i begins, and to the upper floors.
    Practical tip
    The best side to start Sa‘i after tawaf. Electric scooter service for Sa‘i is available on the Safa side.
  • Gate 13Abu Qubais GateEast side · near Safa, Mount Abu Qubais sideباب أبي قبيس+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after Mount Abu Qubais, which rises east of the Kaaba; today the Al-Safa Palace stands on it.
    Location and nearby
    On the eastern façade of the Mas‘a near Safa, facing the direction of Mount Abu Qubais.
  • Gate 14Al-Nabi GateEast side · between Safa and Ali Gateباب النبي+
    Meaning of the name
    "The Prophet’s Gate", named after Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him).
    Location and nearby
    On the eastern façade of the Mas‘a, between Abu Qubais Gate and Ali Gate, near Safa. The Nabi Bridge (15) next to it leads to the upper floors.
  • Gate 16Dar al-Arqam GateEast side · Safa side, escalatorsباب دار الأرقم+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after the house of the Companion al-Arqam ibn Abi al-Arqam.
    Location and nearby
    East of the Mas‘a, north of Al-Nabi Gate. Escalators lead up to the upper Sa‘i level.
    History
    Dar al-Arqam stood near Safa. In the early years of Islam the Muslims met secretly in this house and the Prophet taught there. Umar is reported to have embraced Islam there.
  • Gate 17Ali GateEast side · middle of the Mas‘a (green lights)باب علي+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after Ali ibn Abi Talib, the fourth caliph.
    Location and nearby
    On the eastern façade of the Mas‘a between Al-Nabi and Abbas gates; it opens directly onto the green-lit stretch where men walk briskly during Sa‘i.
    History
    It carries on the name of an older gate, also known as the Bani Hashim or Batha Gate, which stood elsewhere.
  • Gate –Bab al-SalamEast side · along the Mas‘a, between Safa and Marwahباب السلام+
    Meaning of the name
    "The Gate of Peace".
    Location and nearby
    On the eastern façade of the Mas‘a, between Ali Gate and Abbas Gate.
    History
    Also known as the Gate of Bani Shaybah, the family that has kept the key of the Kaaba for centuries. Entering through it for the arrival tawaf is an old tradition. The historic Bani Shaybah arch inside the mataf was removed in the 1960s to widen the tawaf area.
    Practical tip
    Its number is shown on the gate sign.
  • Gate 20Al-Abbas GateEast side · direct access to the Mas‘aباب العباس+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib, the Prophet’s uncle.
    Location and nearby
    On the eastern façade of the Mas‘a between Ali Gate and Bani Shaybah Gate; it has three portals, the large one in the middle.
  • Gate 21Bani Hashim GateEast side · bridge to the upper floorsباب بني هاشم+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after the Banu Hashim, the Prophet’s clan of Quraysh.
    Location and nearby
    On the eastern façade of the Mas‘a, north of Abbas Gate. It is a bridge gate giving access to the upper floors.
  • Gate 22Bani Shaybah GateEast side · near Marwahباب بني شيبة+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after the Bani Shaybah, the family that holds the key of the Kaaba.
    Location and nearby
    On the eastern façade of the Mas‘a near Marwah; opens onto the Sa‘i gallery.
    History
    A newer gate that carries the name of the historic Bani Shaybah arch in the mataf, which no longer exists.
  • Gate 23Al-Marwah GateNortheast · Marwah hill, end of Sa‘iباب المروة+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after Mount Marwah. "Marwah" refers to white, shiny pebbles.
    Location and nearby
    The closest gate to Marwah, at the northern end of the Mas‘a, with escalators to the upper floors.
    Practical tip
    Sa‘i ends at Marwah, so this is the nearest exit afterwards, e.g. to have your hair cut.
  • Gate 28Quraysh GateNorth · northern end of the Mas‘a, Marwah sideباب قريش+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after Quraysh, the Prophet’s tribe.
    Location and nearby
    At the northern end of the Mas‘a, west of Marwah Gate; gives access to the Marwah area.
  • Gate 45Al-Fath GateNorth · between Marwah and King Abdullah Gateباب الفتح+
    Meaning of the name
    "The Gate of Conquest", recalling the Conquest of Mecca (8 AH / 630 CE).
    Location and nearby
    On the northern side of the Haram, west of Marwah. One of the five main gates, with two minarets.
  • Gate 62Umrah GateWest · Jabal Omar side, direct access to the matafباب العمرة+
    Meaning of the name
    "The Umrah Gate".
    Location and nearby
    On the west-northwest side, facing the Jabal Omar area. One of the five main gates; the ground floor leads directly to the mataf.
    Practical tip
    One of the gates assigned for Umrah pilgrims in ihram to enter the mataf; a good choice for tawaf.
  • Gate 74Abu Bakr GateWest-southwest · north of King Fahd Gateباب أبي بكر+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after the first caliph, Abu Bakr al-Siddiq.
    Location and nearby
    A single-portal gate on the western façade of the King Fahd extension, close to King Fahd Gate.
  • Gate 79King Fahd GateWest-southwest · Jabal Omar hotels sideباب الملك فهد+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after King Fahd; built during his major expansion (1982–1988).
    Location and nearby
    On the west-southwest façade, facing the Jabal Omar hotels. One of the five main gates, with three arches and two minarets.
    Practical tip
    One of the gates assigned for Umrah pilgrims to enter the mataf. The stairs at gate 78 next to it lead to the upper floors.
  • Gate 84Jabir ibn Abdullah GateSouthwest · used as a women’s entranceباب جابر بن عبد الله+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after the Companion Jabir ibn Abdullah, a prolific narrator of hadith.
    Location and nearby
    At the southwestern corner, between King Fahd Gate and King Abdulaziz Gate.
    Practical tip
    Built in the King Fahd expansion and known as a women’s gate. This can change; check the signs.
  • Gate 94Ibrahim GateSouth · west of King Abdulaziz Gateباب إبراهيم+
    Meaning of the name
    Carries the name of the historic "Bab Ibrahim" of the Haram.
    Location and nearby
    On the southern façade close to King Abdulaziz Gate; easy to reach from the Clock Tower hotels.
  • Gate 100King Abdullah GateNorthwest · King Abdullah expansionباب الملك عبد الله+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after King Abdullah, under whom the northern expansion began.
    Location and nearby
    On the northwest side; the central entrance of the King Abdullah expansion. A large gate with three arches and two minarets, flanked by smaller gates.
    Practical tip
    Opens onto the large prayer halls of the new expansion. It is far from the Kaaba; for tawaf prefer the Umrah or King Fahd gates.

Masjid an-Nabawi21 gates

  • Gate 1Bab al-SalamWest wall, south end · entry for visiting the Rawdah and the Prophet’s graveباب السلام+
    Meaning of the name
    "The Gate of Peace"; visitors enter here to greet the Prophet with salam.
    Location and nearby
    At the southern end of the western wall of the old (Ottoman) mosque. Walking along the qibla wall from here leads to the Rawdah and in front of the Prophet’s grave.
    History
    One of the oldest and most decorated gates. Its Ottoman façade still survives behind the modern one.
    Practical tip
    For the visit to the Prophet’s grave you enter here and leave through Al-Baqi Gate (41). Praying in the Rawdah requires a Nusuk app permit.
  • Gate 2Abu Bakr al-Siddiq GateWest wall · just north of Bab al-Salamباب أبي بكر الصديق+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after Abu Bakr al-Siddiq; also called "the khawkha (small door) of Abu Bakr".
    Location and nearby
    On the western wall of the old mosque, between Bab al-Salam and Bab al-Rahmah.
    History
    According to al-Bukhari, shortly before his death the Prophet ordered all doors opening into the mosque to be closed except the door of Abu Bakr. This gate preserves that memory.
  • Gate 3Bab al-RahmahWest wall · one of the oldest gatesباب الرحمة+
    Meaning of the name
    "The Gate of Mercy"; its old name is Bab Atikah.
    Location and nearby
    On the western wall of the old mosque, north of Abu Bakr Gate.
    History
    Known as one of the three gates the mosque had in the Prophet’s time.
  • Gate 4Al-Hijrah GateWest · next to Bab al-Rahmahباب الهجرة+
    Meaning of the name
    Recalls the Prophet’s Hijrah from Mecca to Medina (622).
    Location and nearby
    Right next to Bab al-Rahmah, opening onto the western courtyard.
  • Gate 5Quba GateSouthwest · towards Qubaباب قباء+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after Quba, about 5 km south, home of the first mosque in Islam.
    Location and nearby
    On the southwestern wing of the King Fahd extension; triple-arched.
  • Gate 7–9King Saud GateWest façade · southwest cornerباب الملك سعود+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after King Saud ibn Abdulaziz.
    Location and nearby
    At the southern end of the western façade of the King Fahd extension, with two large and five smaller portals.
  • Gate 11Al-Aqiq GateWest façade · between King Saud and Majidi gatesباب العقيق+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after Wadi al-Aqiq west of Medina, where several Companions lived.
    Location and nearby
    In the middle of the western façade of the King Fahd extension.
  • Gate 12–14Al-Majidi GateNorthwest · women’s section entranceباب المجيدي+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after the Ottoman Sultan Abdulmejid I, who had the mosque largely rebuilt.
    Location and nearby
    On the northwestern wing of the King Fahd extension, with two large and five smaller portals.
    Practical tip
    Used as an entrance to the women’s section.
  • Gate 16–18Umar ibn al-Khattab GateNorth wall, west end · women’s sectionباب عمر بن الخطاب+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after the second caliph, Umar ibn al-Khattab.
    Location and nearby
    At the western end of the north wall. Entrances 16 and 17 lead to the women’s section; the women’s library is nearby.
  • Gate 19Badr GateNorth wall · between Umar and King Fahd gatesباب بدر+
    Meaning of the name
    Recalls the Battle of Badr (624), the first major victory of the Muslims.
    Location and nearby
    A single-portal gate on the north wall, between Umar Gate and King Fahd Gate.
    History
    Above the gate the verse "Enter it in peace, secure" (al-Hijr 15:46) is carved in marble.
  • Gate 20–22King Fahd GateNorth façade · main entranceباب الملك فهد+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after King Fahd, who greatly expanded the mosque.
    Location and nearby
    The main entrance in the middle of the north façade and the largest gate: two minarets, seven doors and five domes above.
    Practical tip
    The easiest entrance from the northern hotels. Use the gate number as a meeting point.
  • Gate 23Uhud GateNorth wall · women’s section, towards Uhudباب أحد+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after Mount Uhud and the Battle of Uhud (625).
    Location and nearby
    On the north wall between King Fahd Gate and Uthman Gate, facing Mount Uhud.
    Practical tip
    Used as an entrance to the women’s section.
  • Gate 24–26Uthman ibn Affan GateNortheast · women’s sectionباب عثمان بن عفان+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after the third caliph, Uthman ibn Affan.
    Location and nearby
    At the eastern end of the north wall, with two large and five smaller portals; opens directly to the northeastern women’s section.
    Practical tip
    Used as an entrance to the women’s section.
  • Gate 28–30Ali ibn Abi Talib GateEast façade, north end · women’s sectionباب علي بن أبي طالب+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after the fourth caliph, Ali ibn Abi Talib.
    Location and nearby
    At the northern end of the eastern façade; one of the largest gates.
    Practical tip
    Used as an entrance to the women’s section.
  • Gate 33–35King Abdulaziz GateEast façade, southeast corner · Baqi sideباب الملك عبد العزيز+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after King Abdulaziz, founder of Saudi Arabia.
    Location and nearby
    At the southeast corner of the King Fahd extension, opening onto the eastern courtyard towards Jannat al-Baqi.
  • Gate 37Makkah GateSouth (qibla) wall · towards Meccaباب مكة+
    Meaning of the name
    Named "Makkah Gate" because it faces Mecca (the qibla).
    Location and nearby
    On the southern (qibla) wall of the King Fahd extension; triple-arched.
  • Gate 38Bilal GateSoutheast · east of Bab al-Nisaباب بلال+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after Bilal ibn Rabah, the Prophet’s muezzin.
    Location and nearby
    In the southeast, between Bab al-Nisa and Makkah Gate.
  • Gate 39Bab al-NisaEast wall · near the Prophet’s graveباب النساء+
    Meaning of the name
    "The Women’s Gate".
    Location and nearby
    On the eastern wall of the old mosque, north of Bab Jibril.
    History
    According to Abu Dawud, the Prophet said "If only we left this door for the women", and Ibn Umar never entered through it until he died. The gate was opened in the time of Umar.
  • Gate 40Bab JibrilEast wall · next to the Prophet’s chamberباب جبريل+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after the angel Gabriel; also called "Bab al-Nabi" as the Prophet used to enter through it.
    Location and nearby
    On the eastern wall of the old mosque between Al-Baqi Gate and Bab al-Nisa, very close to the Prophet’s chamber.
    History
    Tradition holds that Gabriel came to the Prophet through this gate, hence the name. It was moved eastward during the expansions.
  • Gate 41Al-Baqi GateEast wall · visit exit, towards Jannat al-Baqiباب البقيع+
    Meaning of the name
    Named after Jannat al-Baqi cemetery opposite it.
    Location and nearby
    At the southern end of the old mosque’s eastern wall; the closest gate to the Prophet’s grave, facing Jannat al-Baqi. It is aligned with Bab al-Salam.
    Practical tip
    You exit here after visiting the Prophet’s grave; Jannat al-Baqi is easy to visit afterwards.
  • Gate 42Bab al-A’immahSouth (qibla) wall · reserved for the imamsباب الأئمة+
    Meaning of the name
    "The Imams’ Gate", used by the imams; also called Bab al-Janaiz.
    Location and nearby
    On the qibla wall of the old mosque, opening near the Ottoman mihrab.
    Practical tip
    Not open to visitors.

Transport

Getting around Makkah and Madinah, buses, reaching the Haram and practical tips.

Fares, timetables and routes change often, especially in Ramadan and Hajj. Check the operator's app before you travel. Values marked "approx." are not exact.

Makkah

  • Network: Makkah Bus links most of the city to terminals around the Haram on 12 routes. Buses run about 22 hours a day, 24 hours in peak season.
  • Fare: Single trip SAR 4, express SAR 8. The app sells day (4 trips) SAR 15, week (16 trips) SAR 58 and month (50 trips) SAR 150 passes (2024 tariff).
  • Tickets: In the "Makkah Bus" app by card or digital wallet, at stop ticket machines, or on board. Do not pay the driver in cash.
  • Terminals: Main terminals around the Haram: T1 Jabal Al-Kaaba (Jarwal), T2 Al-Marwah (Al-Ghazzah), T3 Ibrahim Al-Khalil and T4 Ajyad. Pick the nearest one on the map.

  • Bus: Route 400 runs 24 hours to the Prophet's Mosque area; SAR 3.45.
  • Official taxi: Official taxis outside the terminal have a fixed fare to the Haram area: SAR 75 or 90 depending on vehicle size. The fare board is posted at the exit.

Official taxis+
  • Makkah Taxi: The Royal Commission's official taxi; 47 points including the Haram area and main stations, 24 hours. Metered; cash and credit cards accepted.
  • At the airport: Use only the official taxi rank outside the terminal. Do not go with people offering rides inside the hall.
Sample faresFares are approximate+
  • Jeddah Airport → Makkah: Approx. SAR 150–300.
  • Madinah Airport → Haram: Official fixed fare SAR 75 or 90 (by vehicle size).
  • Makkah hotel → Tan'eem: One way approx. SAR 30–50.
  • Haram → Arafat: About 20 km. No fixed fare; ask for the meter or agree the price first. Vehicle access is restricted during Hajj days.
Ride-hailing apps+
  • Uber, Careem and Rehla are among the apps licensed by the Transport General Authority (TGA). You see the price before the ride and can pay by card.
  • Demand and prices spike after prayers; waiting a few minutes often lowers the price.
Pick-up near the Haram+
  • The area right around the Haram is closed to private cars; taxis and app cars cannot reach the gates.
  • Choose a meeting point on a main road (e.g. Ibrahim Al-Khalil St., Ajyad St.) and share the map pin with the driver.
  • Drivers leave cars at park-and-ride lots at the city edge (e.g. Kudai, Rusaifah) and take the shuttle to the Haram.
Safety and bargaining tips+
  • Meter: By law the driver must start the meter. If it is not on, you do not have to pay.
  • Without a meter, agree the price before getting in and ask whether it is per person or per car.
  • Avoid unmarked private cars ("pirate taxis"), especially at airport and bus station exits.
  • Carry small riyal notes; not every driver accepts cards.
  • Complaints: Transport General Authority complaints line 19929. Note the plate number and time.

Umrah Step by Step

Every step of Umrah from ihram to tawaf, from sa’i to shaving, in a clear and simple guide.

  1. 1

    1. Intention and Ihram

    Dhul-Hulayfah (Miqat)

    • Before reaching the Miqat, perform ghusl or wudu and take care of personal hygiene such as trimming nails and moustache.
    • Men wrap themselves in the two unstitched pieces of Ihram (izar and rida). Women enter Ihram in their normal modest clothing; the face and hands stay uncovered.
    • If it is not a disliked (makruh) time, two rak‘ahs of Ihram prayer are performed.
    • Make the intention for Umrah and recite the Talbiyah. With the intention and Talbiyah you enter Ihram, and the Ihram prohibitions begin.
    Take note+
    • The Miqat must not be passed without Ihram. If it is passed, one must go back and enter Ihram; if one does not go back, a penalty (dam) is due.
    • Dhul-Hulayfah is the Miqat for those coming from the direction of Madinah. Those flying directly from Türkiye enter Ihram on the plane before passing the line of the Miqat.
    • While in Ihram, cutting or shaving hair, clipping nails, applying perfume, hunting and marital intimacy are prohibited. Men do not cover their heads or wear stitched clothing.
    Supplication for this step: Intention for Umrah
  2. 2

    2. Talbiyah

    Road to Makkah

    • The Talbiyah is said at least once when entering Ihram (in the Hanafi school it is a condition of Ihram).
    • Throughout the journey, when going uphill or downhill and when meeting groups of people, the Talbiyah is repeated often.
    • In Umrah, the Talbiyah stops when the Black Stone (Hajar al-Aswad) is saluted (Istilam) at the start of Tawaf.
    Take note+
    • Men say the Talbiyah aloud, women in a low voice.
    • After the Talbiyah it is Sunnah to send blessings on the Prophet and to make supplication.
    Supplication for this step: Talbiyah
  3. 3

    3. Arrival in Makkah and Entering Masjid al-Haram

    King Abdulaziz Gate

    • Check in at the hotel; if possible, rest and perform wudu.
    • Enter Masjid al-Haram through one of its gates (e.g. King Abdulaziz Gate opposite the Clock Tower) with the right foot, reciting the supplication for entering.
    • On first seeing the Kaaba, say takbir and tahlil and make supplication.
    • The greeting of Masjid al-Haram is Tawaf; proceed directly to Tawaf.
    Take note+
    • Before leaving your group, learn the meeting gate (gate number).
    • Put your shoes in a bag and carry them with you; note the gate number.
    Supplication for this step: Supplication for entering the mosque
  4. 4

    4. Tawaf (7 Circuits)

    Kaaba

    • In a state of wudu, come in line with the Black Stone (Hajar al-Aswad) and make the intention for the Umrah Tawaf.
    • Face the Black Stone, raise your hands and perform Istilam; the Talbiyah stops.
    • With the Kaaba on your left, walk 7 circuits outside the Hatim (Hijr Ismail). Each circuit begins and ends in line with the Black Stone.
    • Men perform Idtiba‘ throughout the Tawaf and Raml in the first 3 circuits (a brisk walk with short steps, moving the shoulders).
    Take note+
    • The Umrah Tawaf is a pillar of Umrah. Being in wudu during Tawaf is wajib according to the Hanafi school and a condition according to the Shafi‘i school.
    • If a person’s wudu breaks during Tawaf, they stop, renew their wudu and continue from the circuit where they left off; starting again from the beginning is not required.
    • At Istilam say "Bismillahi Allahu akbar"; saying only "Allahu akbar" is also sufficient.
    • Do not push anyone to kiss the Black Stone; pointing to it with your hand from a distance is sufficient.
    • If in doubt about the number of circuits, go by the lower number.
    • Women do not perform Raml or Idtiba‘.
    Supplication for this step: Between the Yemeni Corner and the Black Stone
  5. 5

    5. Tawaf Prayer

    Maqam Ibrahim

    • After Tawaf, two rak‘ahs of Tawaf prayer are performed behind Maqam Ibrahim (wajib in the Hanafi school).
    • The Sunnah is to recite Surah al-Kafirun in the first rak‘ah and Surah al-Ikhlas in the second.
    • Before the prayer, Idtiba‘ ends and the shoulders are covered.
    Take note+
    • In crowds, praying directly behind the Maqam is not required; it can be performed anywhere suitable in Masjid al-Haram.
    • If it is a disliked (makruh) time, the prayer is performed once that time has passed.
    • Do not block the way of those performing Tawaf.
    Supplication for this step: When approaching Maqam Ibrahim
  6. 6

    6. Zamzam

    Zamzam water points

    • Facing the qibla, saying Bismillah, drink your fill of Zamzam in three sips.
    • Make supplication while drinking; Zamzam serves the intention with which it is drunk.
    • Before going on to Sa'i, perform Istilam of the Black Stone again if possible.
    Take note+
    • The Well of Zamzam lies beneath the mataf, about 20 m east of the Kaaba, and has been closed to visitors since 1964. Zamzam is drunk from the water points and containers found throughout the mosque.
    • Drinking Zamzam is recommended (mustahabb); it is not an obligatory (fard) or necessary (wajib) part of Umrah.
    Supplication for this step: A supplication that may be recited while drinking Zamzam
  7. 7

    7. Sa'i (Between Safa and Marwah)

    Safa

    • Sa'i starts at Safa. Climb Safa, face the Kaaba, raise your hands and say takbir and tahlil and make supplication.
    • Going from Safa to Marwah is the 1st lap, returning from Marwah to Safa is the 2nd. The 7th lap ends at Marwah.
    • Men walk quickly (harwalah) between the green lights (al-Milayn al-Akhdarayn). Women walk normally.
    • Each time you reach Safa or Marwah, face the Kaaba and make supplication.
    Take note+
    • In the Hanafi school, Sa'i is a wajib of Umrah. It is performed after Tawaf.
    • Being in wudu is not a condition, but it is recommended.
    • There is no Idtiba‘ or Raml in Sa'i.
    • You may take a break when tired; it can also be performed in a wheelchair.
    Supplication for this step: Between the green lights (al-Milayn al-Akhdarayn)
  8. 8

    8. Halq / Taqsir and Leaving Ihram (Tahallul)

    Marwah

    • When Sa'i is finished, the hair is shaved or shortened within the boundaries of the Haram.
    • For men, shaving the whole head (Halq) is more virtuous.
    • For shortening (Taqsir), according to the Hanafi school, at least a fingertip’s length (about 1-2 cm) is cut from the ends of the hair on at least a quarter of the head. According to the Shafi‘i school, cutting at least 3 hairs from different parts of the head is sufficient.
    • Women do not shave; they cut a fingertip’s length from the ends of their hair.
    • With Halq/Taqsir you leave Ihram (Tahallul), the Ihram prohibitions end and the Umrah is complete.
    Take note+
    • The Ihram prohibitions continue until Halq/Taqsir; normal clothes are not worn before it.
    • If Halq/Taqsir is done outside the boundaries of the Haram, a penalty is due.
    Supplication for this step: When the Umrah is completed

Supplications

Essential supplications for Umrah in Arabic, with meanings and transliteration.

  1. Intention and Ihram

    Intention for Umrah

    اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أُرِيدُ الْعُمْرَةَ فَيَسِّرْهَا لِي وَتَقَبَّلْهَا مِنِّي

    Transliteration
    Allahumma inni uridul-'umrata fa yassirha li wa taqabbalha minni.
    Meaning
    O Allah! I intend to perform Umrah. Make it easy for me and accept it from me.

  2. Talbiyah

    Talbiyah

    لَبَّيْكَ اللَّهُمَّ لَبَّيْكَ، لَبَّيْكَ لَا شَرِيكَ لَكَ لَبَّيْكَ، إِنَّ الْحَمْدَ وَالنِّعْمَةَ لَكَ وَالْمُلْكَ، لَا شَرِيكَ لَكَ

    Transliteration
    Labbayk Allahumma labbayk, labbayka la sharika laka labbayk. Innal-hamda wan-ni'mata laka wal-mulk, la sharika lak.
    Meaning
    Here I am, O Allah, here I am! Here I am, You have no partner, here I am! Praise, blessings and sovereignty are Yours. You have no partner.
    Source:
    Bukhari, Hajj 26; Muslim, Hajj 19

  3. Arrival in Makkah and Entering Masjid al-Haram

    Supplication for entering the mosque

    بِسْمِ اللَّهِ وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ، اللَّهُمَّ افْتَحْ لِي أَبْوَابَ رَحْمَتِكَ

    Transliteration
    Bismillahi was-salatu was-salamu 'ala Rasulillah. Allahummaftah li abwaba rahmatik.
    Meaning
    In the name of Allah, and blessings and peace be upon the Messenger of Allah. O Allah! Open for me the gates of Your mercy.
    Source:
    Muslim, Musafirin 68

  4. Tawaf (7 Circuits)

    Between the Yemeni Corner and the Black Stone

    رَبَّنَا آتِنَا فِي الدُّنْيَا حَسَنَةً وَفِي الْآخِرَةِ حَسَنَةً وَقِنَا عَذَابَ النَّارِ

    Transliteration
    Rabbana atina fid-dunya hasanatan wa fil-akhirati hasanatan wa qina 'adhaban-nar.
    Meaning
    …Our Lord, give us in this world [that which is] good and in the Hereafter [that which is] good and protect us from the punishment of the Fire.
    Source:
    Al-Baqarah 2:201

  5. Tawaf Prayer

    When approaching Maqam Ibrahim

    وَاتَّخِذُوا مِنْ مَقَامِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ مُصَلًّى

    Transliteration
    Wattakhidhu min maqami Ibrahima musalla.
    Meaning
    And take, [O believers], from the standing place of Abraham a place of prayer.
    Source:
    Al-Baqarah 2:125

  6. Zamzam

    A supplication that may be recited while drinking Zamzam

    اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَسْأَلُكَ عِلْمًا نَافِعًا وَرِزْقًا وَاسِعًا وَشِفَاءً مِنْ كُلِّ دَاءٍ

    Transliteration
    Allahumma inni as'aluka 'ilman nafi'an wa rizqan wasi'an wa shifa'an min kulli da'.
    Meaning
    O Allah! I ask You for beneficial knowledge, abundant provision and healing from every illness.
    Source:
    Narrated from Ibn 'Abbas (may Allah be pleased with him); al-Daraqutni, Sunan, II, 289; al-Hakim, al-Mustadrak, I, 646

  7. Sa'i (Between Safa and Marwah)

    Between the green lights (al-Milayn al-Akhdarayn)

    رَبِّ اغْفِرْ وَارْحَمْ إِنَّكَ أَنْتَ الْأَعَزُّ الْأَكْرَمُ

    Transliteration
    Rabbighfir warham, innaka antal-a'azzul-akram.
    Meaning
    My Lord, forgive and have mercy. Indeed, You are the Most Mighty, the Most Generous.
    Source:
    Narrated from Ibn Mas'ud and Ibn 'Umar

  8. Halq / Taqsir and Leaving Ihram (Tahallul)

    When the Umrah is completed

    الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي قَضَى عَنَّا نُسُكَنَا، اللَّهُمَّ تَقَبَّلْ مِنَّا

    Transliteration
    Alhamdu lillahil-ladhi qada 'anna nusukana. Allahumma taqabbal minna.
    Meaning
    Praise be to Allah, who enabled us to complete our rites. O Allah, accept it from us.
