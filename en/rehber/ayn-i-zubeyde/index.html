Ayn Zubaydah (Zubaydah Aqueduct)

عين زبيدة

A twelve-century-old aqueduct built to bring water to pilgrims, running from Arafat towards Makkah

Overview

Ayn Zubaydah is the historic aqueduct built by Zubaydah bint Ja'far, wife of the Abbasid caliph Harun al-Rashid, to carry water to Makkah and the pilgrimage sites. The water was taken from Wadi Nu'man at the foot of Mount Kara on the Ta'if road, and flowed through the plain of Arafat and along the hills of Muzdalifah towards Makkah. Sources give its length as between 30 and 38 kilometres.

The aqueduct is a channel built of stone and lime mortar, running underground in some places and above ground in others. Access shafts (kharazah) were left about every 50 metres for cleaning and maintenance. Basins were built at Arafat to give water to pilgrims; the remains of these basins near Jabal al-Rahmah, and channel walls rising like a fortress near Muzdalifah, can still be seen.

The aqueduct is dry today. Its endowment is administered by the General Authority for Awqaf, and the Royal Commission for Makkah City and Holy Sites is working to preserve and present its remains.

History

Zubaydah bint Ja'far was born in 145 AH (762); she was the wife of Harun al-Rashid and the mother of the caliph al-Amin. It is reported that on her pilgrimages she saw the water shortage suffered by the people of Makkah and the pilgrims and decided to build an aqueduct. The sources give different years for its construction, generally the early 9th century. According to the reports, Zubaydah told the engineers to finish the work whatever the cost, and the expense reached 1.7 million mithqals of gold.

Water was first brought from Wadi Hunayn. When its water declined, the springs of Wadi Nu'man were also connected and the channel was extended to Arafat, so that pilgrims could find water at Arafat on the Day of Arafah. Zubaydah also built wells and cisterns along the pilgrim road from Baghdad to Makkah, known as Darb Zubaydah. She died in Baghdad in 216 AH (831).

The aqueduct was repaired many times over the centuries. Under the Ottoman sultan Suleiman the Magnificent a major restoration was carried out and the water was brought into the heart of Makkah; maintenance continued in later Ottoman times. In 1344 AH (1926), when a flood in Wadi Nu'man destroyed much of the channel, King Abdulaziz had it repaired and an Ayn Zubaydah administration was established. After the mid-20th century, as groundwater declined and modern networks were built, the aqueduct fell out of use. In 2024 the Royal Commission for Makkah City and Holy Sites opened a seasonal cultural and walking area called Khasirat Ayn Zubaydah.

Key Events

  1. Early 9th century

    Zubaydah bint Ja'far built the aqueduct bringing water to Makkah and Arafat (sources give different years).

  2. 16th century

    A major restoration was carried out under Suleiman the Magnificent.

  3. 1344 AH (1926)

    A flood destroyed the channel; King Abdulaziz had it repaired.

  4. 2024

    The Khasirat Ayn Zubaydah cultural and walking area opened seasonally.

Religious Significance

No special worship or virtue is reported for this aqueduct. Its importance comes from being a great charitable work built to bring water to pilgrims, a form of ongoing charity (sadaqah jariyah).

The Prophet taught that when a person dies their deeds come to an end, except for ongoing charity, beneficial knowledge and a righteous child who prays for them. This work, which quenched the thirst of millions of pilgrims over the centuries, is a living example of that hadith. It is fitting here to remember Zubaydah and all who laboured to keep it alive with good words and prayers.

Etiquette and Tips

  • The remains are most easily seen on tours of Arafat and Muzdalifah; ask your guide to point them out.
  • Do not climb the historic walls, move their stones or enter the channel.
  • There is little shade in the open; take water and a hat, and avoid the midday heat.

Visiting Note

Saudi regulations

The remains of the aqueduct lie in open country, and most are protected. The Khasirat Ayn Zubaydah area opens seasonally; check through official channels whether it is open and what the visiting conditions are before you go. Access to the area of the holy sites is restricted during the Hajj season.

Regulations may change; check the latest rules before your visit.
