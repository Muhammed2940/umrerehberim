Masjid al-Qiblatayn (Mosque of the Two Qiblahs)

مسجد القبلتين

Where the qiblah was turned to the Kaaba

Overview

Masjid al-Qiblatayn lies north-west of Madinah, on the volcanic ground known as the Harrat al-Wabarah, about 5 km by road from the Prophet's Mosque. It was first called the Mosque of Banu Salimah, after the tribal area where it stands. While the Prophet was leading the noon or afternoon prayer here, verse 144 of Surat al-Baqarah was revealed and the qiblah was turned from al-Masjid al-Aqsa in Jerusalem to the Kaaba in Makkah. Hence the mosque received its present name, meaning “the mosque of the two qiblahs”.

The present building was enlarged and rebuilt by the Saudi authorities in 1987. With an area of about 3,920 m², two domes and two minarets on the qiblah side, it is a large mosque. The monumental thuluth and Kufic calligraphy inside is the work of the Turkish calligrapher Hasan Çelebi.

History

After his emigration to Madinah, the Prophet prayed facing Jerusalem for about sixteen or seventeen months. As the Qur'an relates, he longed for the Kaaba to be the qiblah and would turn his face towards the sky. With verse 144 of Surat al-Baqarah the qiblah was turned to the Kaaba. The sources relate that this happened in the mosque in the territory of Banu Salimah, during the prayer itself, and that the congregation turned towards the Kaaba without breaking their prayer. From then on, the title “imam of the two qiblahs” was added to the Prophet's names.

While governor of Madinah, Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz renovated al-Qiblatayn together with all the mosques in which the Prophet had prayed. In the Mamluk period, in 1488, its roof was renewed and its courtyard was enclosed with a wall. It was significantly rebuilt under Suleiman the Magnificent (1543-44); the traveller al-Ayyashi, who came here in 1662, describes it as a well-kept mosque among vineyards and gardens. In 1901 Ibrahim Rifat Pasha reported that it was in ruins.

In the Saudi renovation of 1987 a mihrab was built towards the Kaaba, and for a time a panel with translations of the verse was placed on the side of the former qiblah towards Jerusalem; this panel was later removed.

Key Events

  1. 622-623

    After the Hijrah, Muslims prayed facing Jerusalem for about 16-17 months.

  2. 2 AH (624)

    Al-Baqarah 2:144 was revealed and the qiblah was turned to the Kaaba; the mosque was named “al-Qiblatayn”.

  3. 706-712

    Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz renovated the mosque.

  4. 1543-1544

    Major rebuilding under Suleiman the Magnificent.

  5. 1987

    The Saudi authorities enlarged the mosque and rebuilt it in its present form.

Religious Significance

Al-Qiblatayn Mosque is visited because it witnessed a turning point in Islamic history, the change of the qiblah. The Qur'an describes the change of qiblah as a test of who follows the Messenger and tells that the believers submitted to this command at once. The submission of the Companions who turned towards the Kaaba in the middle of their prayer is remembered as a fine example of unconditional obedience to the command of Allah.

There is no authentic narration of a special reward specific to this mosque; as in any other mosque, the prayer of tahiyyat al-masjid is offered here.

Etiquette and Tips

  • On entering the mosque, pray two rak'ahs of tahiyyat al-masjid.
  • Today the mosque has only one mihrab, facing the Kaaba; there may be no separate marker for the direction of the former qiblah.
  • Al-Qiblatayn is usually visited on city tours together with Uhud and the Seven Mosques; it is good to arrive at a prayer time.
  • The women's section is entered through a separate door.
