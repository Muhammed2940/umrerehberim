Quba Mosque

مسجد قباء

The first mosque built by the Prophet

Overview

Quba Mosque lies in the Quba district south of Madinah, about 3 km as the crow flies from the Prophet's Mosque. During the Hijrah, before entering Madinah, the Prophet stayed here among the Banu Amr ibn Awf and built this mosque. It holds a special place in Islamic history as the first mosque that Muslims built openly, in freedom and security.

According to most scholars, the “mosque founded on piety from the first day” praised in verse 108 of Surat at-Tawbah is Quba Mosque, although some say the Prophet's Mosque is meant. The present building was rebuilt in 1985 under King Fahd; with six large domes and four minarets it can hold more than ten thousand worshippers. Its marble was brought from Türkiye, and its calligraphic bands were written by the Turkish calligrapher Hasan Çelebi.

History

Before the Prophet arrived, the first emigrants had turned a date-drying ground in Quba into a place of prayer; Salim, the freed slave of Abu Hudhayfah, led the prayers there. When the Prophet reached Quba he enlarged this place and built the mosque. According to the narrations he laid the first stone of the foundation himself and then asked Abu Bakr, Umar and Uthman to lay stones. The Prophet worked on the construction like a labourer, carrying heavy stones himself. According to a narration in al-Bukhari he stayed in Quba for more than ten nights, and the mosque was built during that time.

After the qiblah was turned towards the Kaaba, the Prophet rearranged the mosque. While governor of Madinah (706-712), Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz rebuilt it in dressed stone and added a minaret. The mosque was repaired many times under the Mamluks and the Ottomans: Suleiman the Magnificent renewed its roof and minaret in 1543, Mustafa II restored it in 1699, and Mahmud II carried out extensive works in 1829.

Enlarged in 1968 under King Faisal, the mosque was completely demolished in 1985 and rebuilt at five times its former size. Today, with its service buildings, it covers a large area.

Religious Significance

The virtue of Quba Mosque is mentioned both in the Qur'an and in the hadith. Verse 108 of Surat at-Tawbah speaks of a mosque founded on piety from the first day, in which there are men who love to purify themselves; most scholars hold that this is Quba Mosque.

Whenever he was in Madinah, the Prophet went to Quba every Saturday, sometimes walking and sometimes riding, and prayed two rak'ahs there. He gave the glad tidings that whoever purifies himself at home and then comes to Quba Mosque and prays there will have a reward like that of an Umrah. Umar also used to visit Quba and showed it great respect.

Etiquette and Tips

  • In line with the hadith, make wudu at your hotel before coming and pray two rak'ahs in the mosque.
  • If possible, visit on a Saturday; it is good to go with the intention of following the Sunnah.
  • There is a pedestrian route of about 3 km between the Prophet's Mosque and Quba; it can be walked in the cooler hours.
  • There is a separate entrance and prayer area for women; follow the signs.
  • There are shops selling dates and souvenirs around the mosque; do your shopping outside prayer times.
