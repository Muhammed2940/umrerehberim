Mount Thawr and its Cave

جبل ثور وغار ثور

The three-night refuge on the road of the Hijrah

Overview

Mount Thawr is a high, rocky mountain south of Masjid al-Haram. The cave in which the Prophet and Abu Bakr hid for three nights during the Hijrah is a small natural hollow beneath a large rock, near the summit on the slope facing Makkah.

The cave has two openings, one to the west and one to the east. The western opening, through which the Prophet entered, is narrow and slightly above the ground, while the eastern one is large enough for a person to pass through easily. Today the city of Makkah has spread up to the northern foothills of the mountain.

History

As Muslims began to emigrate to Madinah, the Makkan polytheists met at Dar al-Nadwah and decided to kill the Prophet. Learning of this, the Prophet planned the emigration at the house of Abu Bakr. Abdullah ibn Urayqit, who knew the routes well, was hired as a guide and asked to bring the camels to the foot of Thawr three days later. Ali slept in the Prophet's bed to mislead the polytheists.

At midnight the Prophet and Abu Bakr climbed Mount Thawr, in the opposite direction from the Madinah road, and took refuge in the cave. Since the pursuers would search mostly along the road to Madinah, this choice was meant to mislead them. A group of polytheists, led by trackers, came right up to the mouth of the cave. When Abu Bakr grew anxious, the Prophet reassured him: "Do not grieve; Allah is with us." This moment is described in verse 40 of Surah al-Tawbah.

For three nights Abu Bakr's son Abdullah gathered news in Makkah by day and brought it in the evening. The shepherd Amir ibn Fuhayrah drove his sheep near the cave, providing milk and erasing the footprints. On the morning after the third night the guide brought the camels and the party set out for Madinah. Some of the well-known narrations, such as the spider spinning a web and doves nesting at the entrance, have been criticised by hadith scholars for their chains of transmission.

Key Events

  1. 622

    The polytheists decide at Dar al-Nadwah to kill the Prophet; the emigration is planned at Abu Bakr's house.

  2. Night of the Hijrah

    Ali sleeps in the Prophet's bed; the Prophet and Abu Bakr take refuge in the Cave of Thawr.

  3. Three nights

    The pursuers reach the mouth of the cave but leave without looking inside. "Do not grieve; Allah is with us."

  4. 1 Rabi al-Awwal / September 622

    The guide Abdullah ibn Urayqit brings the camels and the party sets out for Madinah.

Religious Significance

The Cave of Thawr is a symbol of the Hijrah and of Allah's protection of His Messenger. Describing this moment, the Qur'an refers to Abu Bakr as "the second of two"; for this reason he is known in Turkish and Persian literature as the "friend of the cave".

Visiting the cave is not a religious obligation. It is a place that reminds us of trust in Allah in the hardest moments, of taking every precaution, and of loyal friendship.

Etiquette and Tips

  • The climb up Thawr is longer and harder than Hira; go only if you are fit, and with a guide or a group.
  • Start before sunrise or early in the morning; never climb in the midday heat.
  • Take plenty of water, a hat and a torch; the rocks may be slippery on the way down.
  • Wait your turn at the cave; do not write on the rocks or leave litter.
  • For those who cannot climb, seeing the mountain from below and reflecting on the Hijrah is enough.

Visiting Note

Saudi regulations

There are no official visitor facilities on the mountain; one way can take 1.5–2 hours and the path is poorly maintained in places. The authorities may restrict climbing at times for safety reasons.

Regulations may change; check the latest rules before your visit.
