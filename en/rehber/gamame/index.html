Al-Ghamamah Mosque

مسجد الغمامة

The Eid prayer ground; with the mosques of Abu Bakr and Ali nearby

Overview

Al-Ghamamah Mosque lies about 500 metres west of the Bab as-Salam gate of the Prophet's Mosque. The Prophet did not lead the Eid prayers in his mosque but in an open area outside the town, the musalla. According to the TDV Encyclopaedia of Islam, al-Ghamamah Mosque was later built in place of the musalla of Madinah; it is therefore also called Masjid al-Musalla.

“Ghamamah” means cloud. According to some narrations, when the Prophet prayed for rain (istisqa) here, the clouds gathered and rain fell, and the mosque is said to take its name from this. The present building dates from the reign of Sultan Abdulmejid (around 1859) and has been restored since. Close by are the Mosque of Abu Bakr and the Mosque of Ali, which share the memory of the same prayer ground.

History

From the 2nd year of the Hijrah, the Prophet led the Eid prayers in this open area west of Madinah, the musalla. The musalla was also the place for large gatherings such as the prayer for rain.

The first mosque here was built while Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz was governor of Madinah (706-712). It was renewed in the 14th century under the Mamluks. The building seen today was built under the Ottoman Sultan Abdulmejid and restored under Abdulhamid II and the Saudi authorities. Rectangular in plan, the mosque consists of an entrance section and a prayer hall covered by six domes.

A few dozen metres north-west of al-Ghamamah stands a small mosque attributed to Abu Bakr, and a little further north another attributed to Ali. It is related that these mosques were built at the places where these Companions led the Eid prayer during their caliphates.

Key Events

  1. 2 AH (624)

    The Eid prayer was prescribed; the Prophet began leading the Eid prayers at the musalla.

  2. According to narrations

    The Prophet came out here to pray for rain and the clouds gathered; the mosque's name comes from this.

  3. 706-712

    Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz built the first mosque on the site of the musalla.

  4. Around 1859

    The present building was built under Sultan Abdulmejid.

Religious Significance

Al-Ghamamah Mosque recalls the musalla where the Prophet led the Eid prayers and shared the joy of Eid with his community. Praying the Eid prayer in an open area with a large congregation is his Sunnah.

The narration about the prayer for rain reminds us of the importance of turning to Allah and seeking His mercy in times of hardship. No authentic hadith is known about a special reward specific to this mosque.

Etiquette and Tips

  • It is a few minutes' walk from the western courtyard of the Prophet's Mosque; leave by the Bab as-Salam side.
  • If the mosque is open, pray two rak'ahs of tahiyyat al-masjid; if closed, view it from outside along with the nearby mosques.
  • The mosques of Abu Bakr and Ali are very close; you can see all three on a short walk.

Visiting Note

Saudi regulations

Because of their proximity to the Prophet's Mosque, al-Ghamamah and the small mosques around it have at times been closed for the daily prayers and later reopened; whether they are open may vary.

Regulations may change; check the latest rules before your visit.
