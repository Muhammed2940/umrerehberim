Ji'ranah Mosque

مسجد الجعرانة

Where the Prophet distributed the spoils of Hunayn and entered ihram for Umrah

Overview

Al-Ji'ranah lies between Makkah and Ta'if, outside the boundary of the Haram (in the area called Hill). The Makkan historian al-Azraqi gives its distance from Makkah as 9 miles; according to the TDV Encyclopaedia of Islam it is about 29 km by today's roads. There was once a well of the same name here. Most hadith scholars read the name as al-Ji'irranah, while historians and philologists read it al-Ji'ranah.

Al-Ji'ranah is known as the place where the Prophet distributed the spoils after the Battle of Hunayn and then entered ihram for Umrah. Today a mosque built in memory of these events stands here. Since those inside the Haram must go out to the Hill to enter ihram for Umrah, al-Ji'ranah is, together with al-Tan'im and al-Hudaybiyah, one of the places people go to for this purpose.

History

In the eighth year after the Hijrah (630), shortly after the conquest of Makkah, the tribes of Hawazin and Thaqif suffered a heavy defeat at Hunayn. Some withdrew to Awtas and some took refuge in the fortress of Ta'if. The Prophet left the spoils and captives at al-Ji'ranah, went on to Ta'if and besieged it.

After lifting the siege he returned to al-Ji'ranah but did not distribute the spoils at once; he waited for a while, hoping to return them to the Hawazin if they came as Muslims. When their delegation was late, the spoils were distributed. The Hawazin delegation then came, declared their Islam and asked for the captives; the Prophet obtained the consent of his Companions and returned the captives.

The Prophet gave a larger share to those whose hearts were being reconciled to Islam. When some of the Ansar were hurt by this, he gathered them, explained the wisdom of his decision, praised the Ansar, told them he would always be with them, and prayed for them and their children. The Ansar expressed their regret and said they were content with him.

After the distribution, the Prophet entered ihram at al-Ji'ranah, went to Makkah by night and performed Umrah; he then returned to al-Ji'ranah and set out from there for Madinah. This Umrah is narrated by al-Bukhari and Muslim. A mosque was built here in memory of these events.

Key Events

  1. Shawwal 8 AH (February 630)

    The Battle of Hunayn; the spoils and captives were left at al-Ji'ranah.

  2. 8 AH (630)

    The siege of Ta'if; afterwards the Prophet returned to al-Ji'ranah.

  3. Dhu al-Qa'dah 8 AH (630)

    The spoils were distributed; the captives were returned to the Hawazin delegation; the Prophet addressed the Ansar.

  4. Dhu al-Qa'dah 8 AH (630)

    The Prophet entered ihram at al-Ji'ranah, performed Umrah in Makkah by night, then returned to Madinah.

Religious Significance

Al-Ji'ranah is significant because the Prophet himself entered ihram for Umrah here. Those inside the Haram must go outside it to enter ihram for Umrah. According to the Shafi‘is and some Maliki and Hanbali scholars, al-Ji'ranah is the most virtuous of the places used for this purpose. According to the Hanafis, most Hanbalis and some Shafi‘is, entering ihram at al-Tan'im is more virtuous.

Al-Ji'ranah is also where events took place that show the Prophet's generosity, his care to win his Companions' consent when returning the captives, and his loyalty to the Ansar.

Etiquette and Tips

  • If you will perform Umrah from al-Ji'ranah, take your ihram garments with you; the mosque has ablution facilities.
  • Before entering ihram, two rak‘ahs are prayed; then the intention is made and the Talbiyah recited.
  • Because it is far from central Makkah, go by taxi or group vehicle; plan your return in advance.
  • Recalling the events of Hunayn and Ta'if and the Prophet's address to the Ansar makes the visit meaningful.
  • The schools of law differ on which place is more virtuous; follow your guide and the Diyanet's guidance.

Visiting Note

Saudi regulations

Under Saudi regulations an Umrah permit must be obtained through the Nusuk app. A permit may also be required for a second Umrah while in Makkah; at busy times such as Ramadan, repeat Umrahs may not be permitted.

Regulations may change; check the latest rules before your visit.
