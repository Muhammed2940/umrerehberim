Arafat and Jabal al-Rahmah

عرفات وجبل الرحمة

The plain of the standing, the greatest pillar of Hajj

Overview

Arafat is a wide plain about 21 km east of Makkah on the old road to Ta'if, surrounded on three sides by mountains. It stretches about 6.5 km from east to west and 11-12 km from north to south. It lies outside the boundaries of the Haram, and its limits are marked today with large boundary stones and signs. Namirah Mosque stands on its western edge.

In the north of the plain rises Jabal al-Rahmah, the Mount of Mercy, a small hill of granite boulders, popularly also called Mount Arafat. During the Farewell Pilgrimage the Prophet performed the standing beside the rocks at its foot. Yet the standing may be performed anywhere in Arafat. Much of Arafat has been planted with trees and equipped with roads, water and health facilities for the days of Hajj.

History

The origin of the name Arafat is not known for certain, and the sources give different explanations. According to one narration, while teaching Prophet Ibrahim the rites of Hajj, Jibril asked him here, "Have you learned?", and he replied, "I have learned." It is also narrated that Adam and Eve met and recognised each other here on earth. Others link the name to people meeting one another here and confessing their sins in search of forgiveness.

In pre-Islamic times the Quraysh, regarding themselves as the people of the Haram, did not stand at Arafat, which lies outside the Haram, but at Muzdalifah. Islam abolished this privilege, and all pilgrims stood at Arafat. In the tenth year after the Hijrah, during the Farewell Pilgrimage, the Prophet stood here and, from his camel in the valley of Uranah, delivered the Farewell Sermon, which stressed the sanctity of life, property and honour and the rights of women.

Over the centuries pools and water channels were built at Arafat for the pilgrims; in the Ottoman period water was piped here from the spring of Zubaydah. Today the plain is planted with trees and equipped with many roads to Muzdalifah, car parks and service buildings.

Key Events

  1. Pre-Islamic era

    The Quraysh stand at Muzdalifah rather than Arafat; the other Arabs stand at Arafat.

  2. 9 Dhu al-Hijjah AH 10 (632)

    During the Farewell Pilgrimage the Prophet stands at Arafat and delivers the Farewell Sermon.

  3. The same day

    According to al-Bukhari, the verse "This day I have perfected your religion for you" (al-Ma'idah 5:3) is revealed at Arafat on a Friday.

  4. Ottoman period

    Pools filled by pipeline from the spring of Zubaydah serve the pilgrims during Hajj.

  5. Today

    The plain has been planted with trees; many roads and the Mashaer train link Arafat with Muzdalifah.

Religious Significance

Standing at Arafat is the most important pillar of Hajj; the Prophet said, "Hajj is Arafah." A pilgrim who is not present at Arafat for at least a moment during the standing time has no valid Hajj. The standing begins after midday on the ninth of Dhu al-Hijjah (the Day of Arafah) and lasts until dawn of the day of Eid; remaining at Arafat until sunset is obligatory.

The Prophet said that there is no day on which Allah frees more of His servants from the Fire than the Day of Arafah. Arafat is a place of supplication, repentance and forgiveness. Seeing Arafat during an Umrah trip is not an act of worship, but it is a fine opportunity to reflect on Hajj and the Farewell Sermon.

Etiquette and Tips

  • When visiting Arafat, remember the Farewell Sermon and the standing; spend your time in supplication and seeking forgiveness.
  • Climbing Jabal al-Rahmah is not an act of worship; if you climb, be careful on the rocks and do not push in crowds.
  • Do not write on the white pillar at the top or tie strings or cloth to the rocks.
  • Carry a hat or umbrella and water against the sun; avoid the midday hours in summer.

Visiting Note

Saudi regulations

Outside the days of Hajj, Arafat can be reached by bus or taxi; groups usually visit Arafat, Muzdalifah and Mina together on a Makkah ziyarah tour. During the Hajj season only those holding a Hajj permit (tasrih) may enter the area.

Regulations may change; check the latest rules before your visit.
