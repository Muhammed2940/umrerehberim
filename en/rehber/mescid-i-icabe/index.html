Masjid al-Ijabah

مسجد الإجابة

The Mosque of Banu Mu'awiyah, where the Prophet made three supplications

Overview

Masjid al-Ijabah lies about 385 m north of al-Baqi', north-east of the Prophet's Mosque and about 600 m from it. Because it stands in the territory of the Banu Mu'awiyah ibn Malik clan of the Aws tribe of the Ansar, its first name was the Mosque of Banu Mu'awiyah. It came to be called the Mosque of “al-Ijabah” (the answering) because of the hadith in Sahih Muslim relating that the Prophet prayed here, made a long supplication, and that two of his requests were granted.

The present building was rebuilt on the site of the old one in 1418 AH (1997-98) by the Saudi Ministry of Awqaf. With an area of about 1,000 m², one dome and a single minaret overlooking its surroundings, it is a medium-sized mosque. It is open for the daily prayers.

History

The mosque already existed in the time of the Prophet, in the territory of Banu Mu'awiyah. According to the narration of Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, one day the Messenger of Allah, returning from al-Aliyah, the upper part of Madinah, stopped at this mosque and prayed two rak'ahs, and the Companions prayed with him. Then he supplicated to his Lord at length, and turning to them he said that he had asked three things for his Ummah, that two had been granted and one withheld (Muslim, Fitan).

The Madinan historian Ibn Shabbah (d. 262/876) counts it among the mosques in which the Prophet prayed. It is recorded as being in ruins in the seventh and eighth centuries AH; as-Samhudi (d. 911/1506) reports that it was restored towards the end of the ninth century AH. The later traveller al-Ayyashi speaks of a building with a minaret. Some sources also call it the “Mosque of al-Mubahalah”, linking it to the mutual invocation proposed to the delegation of Najran, but this connection is not certain.

Under the Saudi administration the old building was demolished and the present, larger mosque was built in 1418 AH (1997-98).

Key Events

  1. The Prophet's time

    The Prophet prayed two rak'ahs here and made three supplications for his Ummah; two were granted.

  2. 7th-8th centuries AH

    The mosque is recorded as being in ruins.

  3. Late 9th century AH

    According to as-Samhudi, the mosque was restored.

  4. 1418 AH (1997-98)

    The Saudi Ministry of Awqaf rebuilt and enlarged the mosque.

Religious Significance

Masjid al-Ijabah carries the memory of the Prophet's deep compassion for his Ummah. Here the Messenger of Allah asked that his Ummah not be destroyed by famine and not be destroyed by drowning (a flood), and these two prayers were granted. His prayer that Muslims not fall into conflict among themselves was not granted.

This hadith reminds us how important it is to preserve the unity of the Ummah and not to let disagreements turn into conflict. No special reward has been narrated for this mosque; yet in a place where the Prophet supplicated, praying to Allah for his Ummah is a beautiful act of remembrance.

Etiquette and Tips

  • On entering the mosque pray two rak'ahs of tahiyyat al-masjid and supplicate as you wish.
  • Remembering that the Prophet prayed here for his Ummah, it is good to pray for the unity and peace of the Muslims.
  • It is a short walk from the Prophet's Mosque and al-Baqi'; you can stop by after visiting al-Baqi'.
  • It may be closed outside prayer times; it is better to go close to a prayer time.
